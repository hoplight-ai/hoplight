What you buy

Name the unit, then the number.

Hourly billing makes you pay for our learning curve, and it turns every good idea into a scope argument. So the unit comes first. Which one applies depends on what kind of organization you are, and that is a five-minute conversation.

Labor institution

An annual, per-tenant license to Rayli. Your procurement already understands licenses and your budget cycle is already annual. You are buying capability, not a project with an end date.

Political or advocacy organization

A fixed-fee project with one named deliverable. You are running a campaign, not a program. An hourly quote invites a scope argument you have no time to have.

Foundation or intermediary

A fixed-fee engagement, through a fiscal sponsor where the money is restricted. Grant dollars move in grant-shaped amounts, so the engagement is shaped to match.

Cohort or convening

A stipend that covers the sessions, plus office hours invoiced separately. This structure is already proven and already delivered.

Funder of public infrastructure

Monthly sponsorship covering hard costs plus margin. You are paying for the thing to keep existing and stay current, which is a different purchase from a build.

The transfer line

You are not renting us forever.

Most consulting engagements end with a system only the consultant can operate. That is not an accident, it is the business model. Ours is quoted differently, at the front, in the original scope.

01

Build

The system exists and does the work it was scoped to do. Fixed price, named deliverable, agreed before anyone starts.

02

Transfer

A runbook a competent stranger can execute without asking a question. Credentials and accounts moved into your name. One full operating cycle supervised with your person driving. A written escalation path that says what comes back to us and what does not.

03

Stewardship

Our judgment on the system after you are the one running it. Priced openly, optional, and never the mechanism that keeps you dependent.

Transfer quoted at the end of an engagement is a change order nobody approves. Quoted at the front, it is a deliverable.
